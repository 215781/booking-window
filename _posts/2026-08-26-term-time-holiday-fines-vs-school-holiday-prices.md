Every year the same sum does the rounds in school-gate WhatsApp groups: the holiday costs £2,500 more in half-term, the fine is £80, so why not go a week early? We track family holiday prices every morning, so we can put real numbers on one side of that sum. The other side is the law, and it is less simple than £80.

This is not legal advice, and we are not encouraging anyone to take children out of school. Here are the facts on both sides, and the lawful ways to cut the school-holiday premium.

> **The short answer:** in England a term-time holiday fine is **£80 per parent, per child** (£160 if paid late, and £160 for a second fine within three years). For two parents and two children that is **£320** at the lowest rate. In our checks the school-holiday premium on a family week was much bigger: about **£2,400** on a Club Med ski week at February half-term and **£2,800 to £3,100** on a Mark Warner beach week in August. But the fine is not a fee you pay to make the absence acceptable, and the costs climb fast if it happens again.

## The fines: what the rules say in England

A national framework for school attendance penalty notices has applied in England since 19 August 2024:

- **When:** a fine can be considered once a pupil has **10 sessions (five school days) of unauthorised absence within 10 school weeks**. A week's holiday meets that.
- **How much:** **£80 per parent, per child** if paid within 21 days; **£160** if paid within 28 days.
- **Second time:** a second notice for the same child within three years is a flat **£160**, with no early-payment reduction.
- **Third time:** there is no third fine. Instead the council considers **prosecution**, where courts can impose much larger fines.
- **Authorisation:** headteachers can only authorise leave in term time in exceptional circumstances. A cheaper holiday does not count.

The "per parent, per child" part is the one people miss. A family with two parents and two children faces four notices: £320 if paid promptly, £640 if paid late, and £640 the next time.

## The premium: what our price checks show

On 25 August 2026, this is what the school-holiday premium looked like for a family of four (two adults, two children):

**Club Med ski, February half-term 2027** (seven nights, no flights): the half-term week cost a median **£2,400 more** than the lowest-priced January week at the same resort, about 27% more. It ranged from £814 extra at [Val Thorens](/club-med/val-thorens/) to £3,222 at [Les Arcs](/club-med/les-arcs/).

**Mark Warner summer 2027** (seven nights, flights from Gatwick): a peak August week cost about **£2,800 to £3,100 more** than late June at [Paleros](/mark-warner/paleros/) and [Phokaia](/mark-warner/phokaia/), and £1,700 to £2,100 more at [Aeolian Village](/mark-warner/aeolian-village/) and [Lemnos](/mark-warner/lemnos/).

So yes: on a single trip, the premium is usually several times the first-time fine. That is why the question keeps coming up.

## Why the sums aren't the whole story

**It is still an unauthorised absence.** Paying the penalty settles the fine, not the absence. It goes on your child's attendance record, and schools take persistent absence seriously.

**The second trip costs double, and the third can mean court.** The framework is built to stop repeat absences. If term-time holidays become your plan rather than a one-off, the "small fine" stops being small.

**The week you would travel instead is not always the week you think.** In February 2027 the week before half-term is the Welsh half-term and the start of the Paris school holidays. At several Club Med resorts it cost as much as, or more than, half-term itself (Alpe d'Huez: £14,352 for the week of 7 February against £11,054 for half-term). Leaving early would have cost more, not less.

**Missing school has a cost that isn't on any invoice.** A week before exams or at the start of a school year matters more than a week in a quiet part of the year, and only you and the school can judge that.

## Lawful ways to cut the school-holiday premium

The better question is where the premium is smallest *inside* the holidays. Our data points to four places:

**1. Pick the school holiday with no premium: Easter 2027.** Easter is early this year (Easter Sunday 28 March), and the French spring holidays come later in April. At 10 of the 11 Club Med Alps resorts we track, the first Easter week cost *less* than the lowest-priced week in mid-March (by a median of about £440 for a family), and at the eleventh it was within £60.

**2. Pick the resort with the smallest premium.** Val Thorens added only £814 for half-term over its lowest January week; Valmorel and La Rosière added around £2,700. If you are tied to half-term, the resort choice is the biggest lever you have.

**3. Choose Christmas over New Year.** New Year cost more than Christmas week at all 11 Club Med resorts we track, usually by £1,000 to £3,500 for a family.

**4. Use the changeover day.** A resort that sells Saturday starts can give you an extra day without missing school, and with Mark Warner each resort has its own changeover day. Make sure the dates you book sit fully inside your school's holidays.

Our [school holidays page](/school-holidays/) shows the price of every holiday week at every resort we track, so you can compare these options in one place.

## The bottom line

The fine is smaller than the premium. That does not make a term-time holiday a sensible money decision, because the rules escalate and the absence does not disappear when you pay. Most families will do better by choosing the cheapest week and resort within the school holidays, and booking it at the right moment.
